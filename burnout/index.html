What happens when "too much" never ends

A state of emotional, physical and mental exhaustion caused by prolonged or chronic stress, especially from work, study or caring for others.
  • Not a medical condition and there's no definitive test, but it has very recognisable signs.
  • Remember the curve. Live on the "too much" side for long enough and burnout is where you end up. (See the Yerkes–Dodson curve.)

In your body

  • Exhaustion and fatigue
  • Sleep problems
  • Muscle aches and headaches
  • Getting ill more often
  • Stomach problems

In how you behave

  • Eating too much or too little
  • Irritability, mood changes
  • Withdrawing socially
  • Emotional detachment
  • Procrastination
  • Unable to concentrate
Work-shaped or life-shaped? A useful test: if your energy and curiosity come back the moment the laptop closes (studying, side projects, hobbies), the problem is probably the job, not you.
Escalate now: if burnout has turned into feeling hopeless, or thinking life isn't worth living, please talk to someone today. Helplines are here.

Common causes at work

Workload & pressure

  • Unmanageable workload
  • Unrealistic expectations
  • Long hours

Control & recognition

  • Little say in how you work
  • Effort goes unrecognised

Environment

  • Toxic culture
  • Unclear role or expectations
  • No manager, no support
  • Unfair treatment

Balance

  • Work spilling into life
  • Job insecurity
Burnout is usually an organisational problem that shows up in a person. Fixing it is not just on you.

Dealing with
burnout

recovery is a
project. give it

Four workstreams for recovery

01 · Prioritise rest & self-care

  • Take time off. Use your leave to fully disconnect, not to catch up on email.
  • Take regular breaks. Stretch, walk, or just step away.
  • Prioritise sleep with a relaxing, screen-free bedtime routine.
  • Eat well. Go easy on sugar, caffeine and alcohol.

02 · Seek support

  • Talk to others: friends, family, colleagues.
  • Consider professional help. A therapist or counsellor can give you real strategies.
  • Talk to your manager. If it's work-related, workload can be adjusted. They can't fix what they don't know about.

03 · Set boundaries

  • Learn to say no to commitments that tip you over.
  • Separate work and life with clear limits on your hours.
  • Disconnect. Schedule time away from all devices.

04 · Re-engage with life

  • Exercise regularly. A 10-minute walk lifts your mood.
  • Make time for hobbies and people that bring you joy.
  • Practise mindfulness: meditation, deep breathing, staying grounded.
Be patient. Recovering from burnout usually takes weeks or months, not a long weekend. Progress counts even when it's slow.

// Pre-incident preparation

go-bag

snacks are a

Pack it before you need it

A go-bag is a list of small, ready-made things that reliably make you feel more human. When you're running low, you don't need to think. You just grab from the bag.

Ideas to steal

  • Call a family member weekly
  • Join a local club (running, climbing, anything)
  • Walk instead of drive
  • Uplifting podcasts and calm music
  • Favourite snacks always to hand
  • Alcohol-free drinks in the fridge
  • A reminder to text a friend
  • A catch-up with a mate
  • Keep your CV up to date. Options feel like control
  • Submit a talk to a community con
